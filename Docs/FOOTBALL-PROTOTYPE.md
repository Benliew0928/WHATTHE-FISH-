Football now uses **Free Ball → attached possession → Kick or successful player tackle → Free Ball**. This replaces free physical dribbling, ordinary character push forces, speed-based ball pushing and the old 0.3-second recapture timer. Match rules and the existing character animations are retained; see [the match flow](FOOTBALL-MATCH.md).

Approach a slow free ball from its front-facing side. Acquisition requires an active grounded athlete who is not airborne, preparing a jump, sliding, knocked down or travelling, no existing controller, a clear stadium-layer sightline, ball speed at most **6.2 m/s**, distance from the athlete at most **1.2 m**, forward half-angle **55°**, and distance from the configured foot anchor at most **0.55 m**. These values and the **0.65 m** foot offset are Inspector fields on [SoccerBall.prefab](../Game/Assets/_Game/Prefabs/Football/SoccerBall.prefab). The foot offset was reduced from 0.9 m for closer control; the existing sphere/capsule/skin-width clearance floor remains in force. Merely touching or passing near another player's held ball cannot transfer possession.

Acquisition selects one `CurrentController`, switches the authoritative ball to kinematic mode, ignores only the owner's capsule collision and immediately places it in front of that athlete's feet. The anchor uses the athlete's current world position and facing, including stationary 180° turns, with ground height sampled at the ball. Movement and turning retain ownership regardless of ball distance or former free-ball speed. No parenting changes, deferred push force, spring, velocity addition or speed-based release is used. The other athletes still cannot climb the ball. A controller's movement keeps the attached sphere inside the existing field/goal limits and respects stadium obstacles; players without the ball can still cross the lines.

Normal walk/run targets are calculated once from the unchanged **4/7 m/s** base speeds using **0.75** (25% slowdown): **3/5.25 m/s**. Only the current controller is slowed. A controller cannot jump or tackle through keyboard, touch or direct simulation requests. Unladen athletes retain the shared jump path. Charging keeps attachment, prevents sprint and uses its existing independent **4 × 0.6 = 2.4 m/s** target rather than compounding the possession multiplier.

Tap/release **F** or **Kick** for a light kick; hold and release for a charged kick. Release follows current facing and repeats grounded/action/range/obstruction checks. Only the controller can kick a held ball; eligible athletes can also kick a free ball. A valid kick clears ownership, restores the owner/ball collision and normal dynamic gravity/CCD/interpolation, then replaces velocity with the existing **12–22 m/s** kick plus **0.25 m/s** upward velocity. There is no accumulated running or previous-ball velocity. The existing 0.35-second kick-action cooldown remains separate from possession.

A slide must actually hit the athlete through the existing `FootballTackle.ResolveContacts`/successful `ReceiveHit` path to release their held ball. Ball-only slide contacts and misses leave attachment intact. A successful hit makes the ball free and uses the independent **8 m/s** horizontal contact setting, never the approximately 28 m/s slide travel speed. The concurrent slide-launch revision supplies **4 m/s** upward velocity; the concurrent tackle revision retains a **0.8 s** hit state. It consumes that slide's single ball contact so repeated callbacks do not accelerate it again. The tackler receives no automatic ownership. The released participants must leave the acquisition area and approach again, then satisfy the normal free-ball eligibility checks; this is a spatial re-entry rule, not a timed recapture cooldown. Other eligible players can receive immediately. Free-ball slides retain the existing limited low-speed deflection and reduced high-speed interception. Slide cooldown is **4 s**; a miss adds **0.8 s at 40% movement speed** after the slide action.

Reset, travel, scene deactivation, despawn and match action gates release ownership and restore ignored collision pairs. Goal reset clears momentum and uses the existing `SnapPose` helper to keep the sleeping interpolated Rigidbody and rendered transform at centre, fixing the invisible-until-contact restart defect. Overtime preserves both free motion and attached possession; match results, selection and scoring stay outside possession logic.

Free balls keep the existing 0.43 kg Rigidbody, 22 cm sphere, gravity, continuous dynamic collision detection, **0.05** linear/angular damping and **2 m/s²** grounded rolling resistance. Ground friction creates visible rotation and the ball slows to sleep. Player interception, no-climb movement, the authored **68 × 105 m** white-line bounds and both **7.32 × 2.44 m** goal openings remain in use. No low global velocity cap or new art assets are introduced.

The host/offline authority alone chooses possession, simulates kicks and resolves tackle release. The existing ball snapshot carries position, rotation, sequence and controller NetworkObject ID at up to 20 Hz. A guest follows the replicated athlete's foot anchor while owned and returns to snapshot interpolation when free. No second network manager or client physics effect is added. **Protocol 17** prevents earlier free-dribbling builds from joining.
