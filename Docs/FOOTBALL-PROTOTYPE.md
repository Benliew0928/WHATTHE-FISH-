# Basic football physics prototype — 2 October 2026

In Football → Explore offline (or a started Football room), approach the centre ball and face it. Press and release **F** or **Kick** for a light kick. Hold to charge, then release to kick once; full charge does not auto-fire. The button displays charge percentage. The kick follows the athlete's facing direction at release. Sliding off the touch button, losing focus, leaving kick range, tackling or leaving Football cancels charging. **E** is tackle; **Space** is jump after merging the shared jump update. The first prototype has no scoring, teams, match timer, bots or kicking animation.

The existing 44 cm visual and 22 cm sphere collider now use a 0.43 kg Rigidbody, gravity, interpolation and continuous dynamic collision detection. The ball starts at 12–22 m/s according to charge, with a small 0.25 m/s upward component. Ground friction creates visible rotation; grounded rolling resistance and small damping reduce speed until it sleeps. Resistance does not act in the air. Solver iterations and angular-speed limits are configured at runtime because these Rigidbody properties are not saved in Unity prefab YAML. CharacterController contacts give bounded horizontal pushes. Free physical movement has no low global speed cap; ordinary pushes bound their resulting speed to the greater of existing speed and the dribble target.

Moving balls are intercepted by every athlete, including their kicker. A short sphere sweep before each physics step stops at an athlete in the ball's path; actual collision entry and incoming controller contacts also remove linear/angular velocity. The nearest world obstacle remains a PhysX collision instead of triggering a player stop through a wall. No team or kicker exclusion applies. Grounded interceptions sleep; airborne contacts retain gravity. An interception advances the existing snapshot sequence so guests snap to the stopped position. Pushing away from a player and subsequent kicks remain available.

Athletes cannot use the football as a step or stand on it. Before controller movement, a horizontal footprint sweep clips movement into the ball and allows movement around its sides, preserving terrain/gravity handling. Existing overlaps separate horizontally instead of lifting the athlete onto the sphere. Bounded pushing uses requested movement speed so pushing from rest still works despite clipped movement. An intercepted ball first stops. A valid front-facing receiver may acquire and dribble it; ineligible receivers cannot immediately re-push during the same contact. Leaving contact or kicking also releases that restriction. The same simulation runs for all server-owned athletes, without team or kicker filtering.

Only the ball is confined to the white-line rectangle plus both authored goal interiors. The Sunvale generator and [source import audit](../ArtSource/Football/Sunvale/unity-import-audit.txt) define a **68 × 105 m** pitch, distinct from its **78 × 115 m** runoff turf. `FootballBall` transforms all eight corners of the authored `Lawn__Pitch` mesh bounds into its stadium frame, so imported mesh rotations and island translation are respected. Authority clamps the ball with its radius included before physics processing and after PhysX impulses in LateUpdate. Outward velocity and corresponding rolling spin are removed; along-line movement and gravity remain. Corners restrict both axes. Guests receive the existing position/sequence snapshot. No boundary collider or player-position restriction is added, so all athletes can cross every line. Both goal mouths are open to the ball: their post colliders define the clear width (7.32 m), the crossbar defines the clear height (2.44 m), and the net mesh defines the rear limit (about 2.20 m deep). The small gap between the white goal line and the goal frame is included. Radius-aware side, rear and sloping roof limits keep the ball inside the net; it can return through the mouth. Outside the mouth, including shots above the crossbar, the goal line still restricts the ball. Scoring and out-of-play rules remain later work.

Kicks require a grounded athlete, no tackle/hit action or travel, a ball within the configurable 1.35 m default and 0.5 m vertical separation, facing toward the ball and an unobstructed stadium-layer line of sight. Successful kicks have a 0.35 s cooldown. Each kick immediately replaces linear velocity and resets spin. Contact callbacks see the updated velocity instead of stacking pending forces; resistance skips the first physics step after a kick/contact.

Offline and host players simulate physics. Guests send an owner-only kick RPC; the host repeats all eligibility checks using its athlete and ball. A server-written snapshot on the host player carries position, rotation, controller NetworkObject ID and reset sequence at up to 20 Hz. Guests have a non-colliding kinematic ball and interpolate snapshots. New exploration, returning from travel, or falling below sea level resets the ball to centre. NGO protocol **13** prevents older player layouts from joining. Internet latency/prediction and physical-phone performance are not qualified by local tests.

Tune [SoccerBall.prefab](../Game/Assets/_Game/Prefabs/Football/SoccerBall.prefab) → `FootballBall` in Inspector. Minimum/maximum kick speeds are 12/22 m/s, maximum charge time 1 s, kick range 1.35 m, linear/angular damping 0.05 and rolling deceleration 2 m/s². These are velocity targets rather than AddForce parameters. Control acquisition requires a grounded player, ball within 1.2 m and a forward half-angle of 55°, horizontal speed ≤6.2 m/s and unobstructed line of sight. Retention uses 1.6 m, 80° and 7 m/s for hysteresis. One current controller, or none, is held on the host; proximity alone cannot transfer it. Actual interception/push, tackle, knockdown, kick, range loss, travel and despawn clear control. Only its owner receives movement targets 3.4/5.95 m/s from the unchanged 4/7 m/s base speeds. Charging replaces this with 4 × 0.6 = 2.4 m/s, ignores sprint and retains control. Release rechecks eligibility. The kicker alone has a 0.3 s auto-recapture exclusion; other players may receive immediately, with normal collision retained.

The acceleration diagnosis found two ordinary push paths (`ConstrainPlayerMotion` and `OnControllerColliderHit`) queuing deferred VelocityChange forces against the same old speed, plus slide displacement feeding an approximately 28 m/s character speed into ordinary pushing. Each actor now pushes at most once per physics tick and writes velocity immediately. Result-vector bounding also prevents two perpendicular pushes from accumulating unintended speed. `FootballTackle.ClaimBallContact` grants one ball hit per slide across all callbacks/frames. Low-speed contact redirects at up to 8 m/s. Faster incoming balls keep 0.65 of incoming velocity plus a limited direction change, bounded below their incoming speed. Different incoming speeds retain different outcomes; no low global speed cap is added. `FootballTackle` exposes 4 s cooldown and 0.35 s extra miss recovery. Slide direction is fixed from launch and normal movement/kicking cannot cancel slide recovery. Existing animations and hit response remain.

Owner-authorized movement input carries charging; stale input clears it. The host owns movement penalties, controller selection and all ball effects. Guests send normalized charge on release; the host repeats eligibility/cooldown checks. Charge elapsed time remains local, so competitive anti-cheat timing remains future work.
