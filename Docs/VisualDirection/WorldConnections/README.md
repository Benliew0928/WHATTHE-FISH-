# One sea, four game islands

Five world-connection concepts · 27 September 2026 · Design exploration

**Selected direction: 03 · Sky-Sail Circuit.** The [working implementation](SKY-SAIL-IMPLEMENTATION.md) now includes group travel and modular assets. See the [actual game gallery](GameReview/index.html) or the [earlier design proposal](SKY-SAIL-PLAN.md). The five original studies remain below for reference.

[Open the visual comparison gallery](index.html) · [Read the full generation prompts](Concepts/20260927/PROMPTS.md)

The aim is a single believable archipelago: standing on one game island, the player can look out and recognize the other destinations across the sea. Each remains a separate playable place. The connection is expressed through composition, scenery and arrival landmarks; inter-island walking is not required.

The boards use the current F1/B1 football and basketball identities and G2/L2 golf and fishing identities as their starting point. Each board contains a world overview and an illustrative player-height view from the fishing shore toward the other three islands. These are generated concept paintings, not game captures or surveyed layouts. Rock heights, landscaping and island proportions are stylized; they do not approve rebuilding the existing game areas.

## 01 · Beacon Bay

![Beacon Bay overview and shore view](Concepts/20260927/01-beacon-bay.png)

The four islands face an open sea court in a loose diamond. A little lighthouse on an isolated rock gives the whole world a common point of orientation. Brass lanterns, timber jetties and sparse buoy lanes repeat at each island and make them feel part of the same place.

From shore, the eye moves from the nearby harbor lantern, across open water and the small lighthouse, to the three different destination silhouettes. The lighthouse is a scenic anchor, not a fifth game or a required transfer hub. Direct island-to-island destination choice remains possible.

**Choose this for:** a coherent resort atmosphere that sits comfortably beside the current art.

## 02 · Ribbon Reef

![Ribbon Reef overview and shore view](Concepts/20260927/02-ribbon-reef.png)

Broad S-shaped ribbons of submerged pale sand and coral braid through turquoise water around the four islands. Deep channels keep every island clearly separate. Matching crescent decks and shell landmarks identify the places where a future journey could start or end.

The sea itself creates the connection. At eye height, nearby clear water, distant color bands and soft foam curves guide attention toward recognizable island silhouettes. The reefs remain submerged and interrupted, so they do not promise a walkable causeway.

**Choose this for:** the strongest balance of originality, open views and the existing tropical style.

## 03 · Sky-Sail Circuit

![Sky-Sail Circuit overview and shore view](Concepts/20260927/03-sky-sail-circuit.png)

Four sail-like shore stations form an aerial circuit over a broad oval of sea. Slender cables and small colorful gondolas give a visible reason for the islands to belong to one resort.

A shore viewpoint includes one local station and a cabin overhead, with the other islands beyond. Keep supports outside sporting areas and limit skyline clutter. This proposes the visual language of travel; it does not require a continuous simulated ride. A future island switch could happen within the station experience.

**Choose this for:** a more playful transport identity and a world that suggests activity between destinations.

## 04 · Tidegate Ruins

![Tidegate Ruins overview and shore view](Concepts/20260927/04-tidegate-ruins.png)

The islands occupy a broad broken crescent around a former sea sanctuary. Small carved limestone gates stand at their arrival shores. Disconnected ribs of submerged ruins suggest a shared history without forming a usable bridge.

From the local gate, the player sees the other destinations across the water. The repeated arch form naturally suggests that these places are linked. Golf retains its larger natural sea arch; crafted gates use distinct carvings, proportions and turquoise inlays.

**Choose this for:** teleportation that feels like part of the setting. The ruins should stay sparse enough to preserve the relaxed sports-island mood.

## 05 · Starfall Sea

![Starfall Sea overview and shore view](Concepts/20260927/05-starfall-sea.png)

The islands form an irregular constellation across a calm twilight sea. Small brass astrolabes mark arrival terraces. Fine trails of starlight and their water reflections connect the destinations visually.

Warm venue lights preserve the football and basketball outlines; pale limestone and the waterfall identify golf. The sky trails remain secondary, leaving plenty of open sky and water. This is a mood proposal, not a decision about particle effects or animation.

**Choose this for:** a memorable magical world identity, especially at dusk.

## Distant-island art direction shared by all five

A future distant replacement should preserve what makes its destination recognizable. Its construction, loading and rendering method are deferred.

| Island | Recognition from another shore | What should survive visual simplification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Football | Long stadium profile with repeated teal canopy scallops | Broad rectangular mass, roof rhythm, cream arcade band, low palm beach |
| Basketball | Compact open bowl with a continuous teal rim | Shorter rounded mass, open center, cream perimeter and warm interior accents where visible |
| Golf | Broad low green landform, limestone shoulders, coastal cascade and natural sea arch | Asymmetric coastline, arch opening, large grass mass, pale waterfall stripe |
| Fishing | Low palm crown and planted limestone shoulders around the inlet | Split rim profile, recognizable palm grouping, teal shelter and arrival landmark |

Fishing's lagoon circle and five inward decks are useful in an aerial map but cannot be the sole distant identifiers at player height. Before committing a layout, compose its outer-shore silhouette from the other islands; strengthen the existing palm groups and limestone profile if needed rather than relying on a readable lagoon interior.

Keep each destination's bearing, coastline orientation and signature landmark positions consistent when visiting different islands. The return view should feel like the same geography. The images establish the intention, not final coordinates.

Each island needs an outward-facing arrival or lookout area with clear views toward the other three destinations. Stadium walls and local terrain can naturally hide parts of the view elsewhere. Compose a local foreground, open middle-distance water, and separated island silhouettes; avoid lining one island up directly behind another.

Use shared sun direction, sea colors and atmospheric haze to unify the world. Reduce fine detail and contrast with distance while retaining each landmark's broad shape. The farthest island should still look intentional, not like an indistinct lump.

## Initial comparison before the user selected Sky-Sail Circuit

**Ribbon Reef** is the strongest starting point for a natural, distinctive seascape. Its water patterns can tie the islands together without filling the horizon with infrastructure.

**Tidegate Ruins** is the clearest alternative if teleportation should be visible in the world's identity. Its shore gates give a natural departure and arrival motif.

If a quieter design is preferred, **Beacon Bay** provides the simplest overall composition. A restrained amount of Ribbon Reef's shallow-water pattern could complement it in a later refinement; no sixth hybrid concept has been generated here.
